Project 3 Write-Up

Our project, California Wildfire Thermal Anomaly Tracker, explores how atmospheric conditions and wildfire activity changed across California from July 24 to August 6, 2024, using NOAA GOES-18 satellite data.

Rationale for Design Decisions

Our main idea was to create one interactive map with overlapping data layers so users could see where wildfires occurred and how atmospheric conditions changed over time.

Originally, water-vapor brightness temperatures were displayed as a grid of blue squares. We later replaced this with smoother D3 contours to make the atmospheric patterns easier to understand. We also expanded our fire detections from the Park Fire region to locations across California.

Visual Encodings

  • Spatial Position: Fire detections are plotted geographically across California, highlighting the Park Fire region.
  • Color Hue: Orange represents fire detections, while blue represents atmospheric conditions.
  • Color Luminance: Different shades of blue represent satellite water-vapor brightness temperatures.
  • Size: Larger orange circles indicate greater fire radiative power.

Interaction Techniques

  • Temporal Filtering: A time slider updates fire detections and atmospheric contours hourly.
  • Animation: Play/pause controls show changes over time.
  • Zooming and Panning: Users can explore different California regions.
  • Details-on-Demand: Hovering reveals fire coordinates and radiative power, while clicking the Park Fire marker opens a detailed comparison graph.

Team Development Process

  • Canyon: Created initial exploratory visualizations, set up the repository, helped prepare the checkpoint video, and worked on data preprocessing.
  • Owen: Created additional exploratory graphs, processed NOAA satellite data, and developed the interactive D3 California map and atmospheric contours.
  • Nathaniel: Contributed to development, testing, and interactive improvements including the slider, zooming, tooltips, and Park Fire comparison graph.

We spent approximately 20 people-hours developing the project. The most time-consuming tasks were satellite data processing, coordinate conversion, implementing D3 interactions, and improving animation performance.

Note: Satellite brightness temperature is not a direct measurement of surface humidity, and observed relationships do not establish causation.
