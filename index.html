Project 3 Write-Up

Our project, California Wildfire Thermal Anomaly Tracker, explores how atmospheric conditions and wildfire activity changed across California from July 24 to August 6, 2024, using NOAA GOES-18 satellite data.

Rationale for Design Decisions

Our main idea was to create one overlaying interactive map because it allows users to see where wildfires occurred and how water vapor conditions changed over time. Originally, the water vapor brightness was displayed through an initial grid of blue squares, but later we turned it into a smoother D3 contour graph to make atmospheric patterns more visually pleasing and easier to understand.

Moreover, we initially focused on a small number of fire detections around the Park Fire region before expanding our dataset to include wildfire detections across California from July 24 to August 6, 2024. Raw satellite data was cleaned and aggregated hourly in Python to convert thermal detections into geographic coordinates and smooth contour fields.

Visual Encodings

  • Spatial Position: Fire detections are plotted geographically across California, highlighting the Park Fire region.
  • Color Hue: Orange represents fire detections, while blue represents atmospheric conditions.
  • Color Luminance: Different shades of blue represent satellite water-vapor brightness temperatures (Kelvin).
  • Size: Larger orange circles indicate greater Fire Radiative Power (FRP, in MW).

Interaction Techniques

  • Temporal Filtering: A time slider updates fire detections and atmospheric contours hourly, synchronized with a vertical reference line on the comparison chart.
  • Animation: Play/pause controls automatically step through hourly changes over time.
  • Zooming and Panning: Users can zoom and pan across different regions of California to inspect localized fire clusters.
  • Details-on-Demand: Hovering over any fire marker reveals its exact Fire Radiative Power (MW), while clicking the Park Fire marker opens a detailed dual-axis time-series comparison graph.

Team Development Process

  • Owen: Developed additional exploratory visualizations, processed NOAA satellite data, built the interactive California wildfire map using D3.js, and added water vapor satellite data to the contour graph.
